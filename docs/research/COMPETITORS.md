# Maploom competitor research

Observed: 2026-11-18. Author: research agent.

## 0. How to read this document (method and limits)

**Important limitation.** In this research session, direct page fetches to every competitor site failed
(the hosts are outside the session's allowed network domains). Everything below therefore comes from
**web-search result snippets** of the competitors' own pages plus third-party reviews, directories and
comparison blogs. No competitor UI was clicked through first-hand.

Consequences:

- Prices are what search snippets showed on 2026-11-18. Snippets can be stale, promo-specific, or
  regional. Each price is labelled with its source type. **Re-verify before using any number in a
  pricing decision.**
- Step-by-step workflows are reconstructed from marketing copy and reviews, not from using the editors.
  Where a step was not seen, it says so.
- Map technology is only stated where a source said it explicitly. Attribution footers ("© Mapbox
  © OpenStreetMap") could not be inspected.
- Several comparison blogs cited here are run by competing map-poster sellers, so their price tables
  about rivals are marketing-adjacent.

Confidence labels used: **[own site]** = snippet from the competitor's own domain; **[3rd party]** =
review, directory or comparison blog; **[dated]** = source is clearly old; **[unverified]** = could not
be confirmed.

To close these gaps, someone with a normal browser should walk through: `littleroads.co/create/address`,
the Mapiful and Grafomap editors, MixPlaces editor, Craft & Oak poster maker, and CartoSketch, and
record the attribution footer, the number of steps to checkout, and the live price grid.

**Legal/ethical note.** Little Roads Co is used here only as a product-flow reference. Maploom must not
copy its branding, artwork, copy, or UI. The same applies to every other company listed.

## 1. Direct competitor: Little Roads Co

- **URL:** https://www.littleroads.co/ (custom flow: `/create/address`; also `/shop/cities/<city>`,
  `/shop/custom/<city>`, `/shop/<state>`)
- **Product:** Custom kids' play mats (rugs) illustrating a real neighborhood or town, for toy-car play.
- **Core feature:** Enter an address and get a "colorful, toy-friendly illustration" of the actual
  neighborhood "complete with roads, houses, and your home highlighted". A second path lets the buyer
  pick a town instead of an exact address; town-level mats are described as including local landmarks.
  [own site]
- **Pricing (USD, observed 2026-11-18 via search snippets of the homepage):**
  - 4' x 6' play mat: **$199**, free US shipping.
  - 5' x 7' play mat: **$299**; described as the size "available for custom designs".
  - A Black Friday promo of $20 off the 4' x 6' size via code appeared in one snippet (date of that
    snapshot unknown).
- **Material:** "premium soft chenille" indoor rug, non-slip backing, hemmed edges; "printed & shipped
  from the USA". Washability/care: **not found**. [own site]
- **Customization options seen:** exact address vs. town; home highlighted; two sizes. Whether the buyer
  can edit landmarks, colors, labels, or the family name: **not found**.
- **Map technology:** **not found** (attribution footer could not be inspected). The terms page states
  mats "are artistic representations and may not be geographically accurate ... designed for play and
  decoration, not navigation". [own site]
- **Workflow (partially observed):** 1) enter address or choose town; 2) [step not seen in snippets];
  3) "Unwrap the Surprise" - preview the design, then the mat is delivered. Pre-made city pages (e.g.
  San Francisco, Los Angeles, Irvine) and state index pages exist as an alternative entry point.
- **Policies:** "Love it or we'll fix it" guarantee; returns cover damage within 14 days and printing
  errors at no cost. [own site]
- **Strengths:** The only vendor found that combines address entry, an automated kid-style illustration
  and a physical play rug in one self-serve flow. Clear single-product story. Simple two-SKU pricing.
  SEO landing pages per city/state and per occasion (e.g. "gifts for kids who just moved").
- **Weaknesses (as far as visible):** One product type only (rug). US-only shipping messaging. No
  independent reviews, founder info or press found, so social proof is thin. No digital download or
  cheaper entry product seen. Accuracy explicitly disclaimed.
- **Opportunities for Maploom:** multi-product output (poster, canvas, puzzle, digital file) from one
  design; non-US markets and metric sizes; visible, editable landmarks; a lower-priced entry product;
  explicit privacy posture; transparent OSM attribution.
- **UX patterns worth noting:** address-first hero; "surprise/gift" framing rather than "map editor"
  framing; programmatic city/state pages for search traffic; occasion pages (moving house).

Sources: https://www.littleroads.co/ , https://www.littleroads.co/terms ,
https://www.littleroads.co/shop/cities/san-francisco-ca , https://www.littleroads.co/gifts-for-kids-who-moved

## 2. Custom map poster companies (the "minimalist line map" norm)

### 2.1 Mapiful

- **URL:** https://www.mapiful.com/
- **Product:** Custom street-map posters (plus other personalized poster types); the best-known brand in
  the category per several comparison guides. [3rd party]
- **Pricing:** Conflicting third-party figures, none read from the live price grid:
  - "from EUR 44.99 per Streetmap poster" plus shipping (2026 comparison blog).
  - "from $45 (poster) to $80 (large format) + frames from $35" (2026 guide).
  - "$59 unframed up to $400+ largest framed" (review blog, undated).
  - $39 (30x40 cm) to $79 (70x100 cm) as of 2020 [dated].
- **Sizes mentioned:** 30x40, 50x70, 70x100 cm (and inch equivalents 11x17 to 24x36).
- **Customization:** location, zoom/pan, style/theme (one source says 100+ themes), size, orientation,
  text labels, frames. One reviewer found the number of templates "overwhelming at first".
- **Map technology:** OpenStreetMap data. A Mapiful team member said in an OpenCage interview that they
  needed their own hosted data source to render maps their way and chose OSM. An OSM Help thread (2018)
  notes the OSM credit is "rather hidden in an FAQ". Mapbox use: **not confirmed**.
- **Workflow (from reviews):** enter location -> choose style -> pick size and orientation -> edit text
  -> checkout.
- **Output:** print only; no digital download per one comparison. [3rd party]
- **Strengths:** brand recognition, large Trustpilot volume (about 2,600 reviews on one regional
  Trustpilot page), mature editor, many themes.
- **Weaknesses:** some Trustpilot reviews call the product "flimsy" for the price; abstract street-line
  aesthetic with no landmarks or illustrated buildings; not designed for children.
- **Opportunity:** Mapiful customers buy a memory of a place. Maploom can sell the same memory with far
  more recognisable content (their house, their school, their park).

## 3. Mapbox-based customizers and AI map-art generators

### 3.1 Maposaic

- **URL:** not confirmed in this session (described by Google Maps Mania, 2021).
- **Product:** Interactive tool for making colourful "mosaic" maps; built on Mapbox vector tiles; users
  recolour the map, download a printable image, and can upload to a public gallery. [3rd party, dated]
- **Pricing:** not found. Open-source status: not confirmed.
- **Relevance:** proof that a single strong visual treatment on top of standard tiles is enough to feel
  like a distinct product; a public gallery works as inspiration and SEO.

Source: https://googlemapsmania.blogspot.com/2021/08/make-your-own-map-posters.html

### 3.2 CartoSketch

- **URL:** https://cartosketch.com/
- **Product:** AI art maps. Product Hunt listing: "transforms real Mapbox geographic data into" art in
  watercolor, hand-drawn, cyberpunk and ink-wash styles, with custom prompts, inpainting and high-res
  export.
- **Pricing:** 1 free credit on signup, no card; paid credit pricing **not found**. A third-party blog
  mentions digital downloads at $19 standard / $29 AI styles for an unnamed AI map tool - attribution to
  CartoSketch is [unverified].
- **Strengths:** real geography plus generative style; inpainting for fixing regions.
- **Weaknesses / unknowns:** geometric fidelity after AI stylisation, text legibility, print resolution
  and print licensing of Mapbox-derived output are all unverified.
- **Relevance:** the closest thing found to "illustrated, non-minimalist map from real data". It
  validates demand, and it marks the main risk for an AI approach: buyers who know the street will
  notice hallucinated roads and houses.

Sources: https://www.producthunt.com/posts/1124895 , https://cartosketch.com/blog/cartosketch-vs-grafomap

### 3.3 Generic AI "map illustration" generators

Vondy (street map illustration generator) and Pixa (sketch/city map generator) produce stylised maps
from **text descriptions**, not real addresses, so the result is fictional geography. Not competitors
for an accurate neighborhood map, but they will appear in the same searches.

Sources: https://vondy.com/street-map-illustration-generator--2e8USboG , https://www.pixa.com/create/city-map-generator

### 3.4 Licensing note for a Mapbox-based route (relevant to our own stack choice)

- Mapbox print attribution: text-only credit "(c) Mapbox, (c) OpenStreetMap" where links are impossible.
- Mapbox docs direct users to contact Mapbox about printing Studio print-export images; a quota of 100
  high-resolution exports per account was mentioned; Japan data in Streets v8+ may only be printed for
  educational or private use.
- OSM: mailing-list and help-forum discussion indicates attribution is expected on printed material
  too; several poster sellers have been criticised for hiding it.

None of this is legal advice and the exact commercial print terms were not found. It does suggest that
rendering directly from OSM data (ODbL) with visible "(c) OpenStreetMap contributors" credit is the
route with the fewest third-party print restrictions. **Get this checked by a lawyer before selling.**

Sources: https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/attribution/ , https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/static-maps/ ,
https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk/2022-January/087249.html ,
https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/20608/commercial-use-osm-in-poster-prints-how-to-credit

## 5. UX references (not competitors)

### 5.1 Felt (felt.com) - map-tool UX

- **What it is:** collaborative browser-based mapping/GIS platform. Not consumer print.
- **Pricing:** free personal tier reported; team/enterprise quote-based per several directories; exact
  current prices **not found**.
- **UX patterns worth borrowing:**
  - Interface in five clear regions: toolbar, legend, detail panel, table, map.
  - Annotations as first-class objects: pins, routes, shapes, text, in one "Add to map" menu.
  - Map-aware drawing: routes and clips snap to roads and boundaries, so non-experts draw clean lines.
  - Viewer mode by default: opening a map cannot accidentally change it.
  - Toolbar moved to the top with dropdowns to give the map the most space (Felt 2.0).
  - Upload anything (GeoJSON, KML, spreadsheets, URLs) with no configuration.
- **For Maploom:** landmarks should behave like Felt annotations - click to select, drag, rename,
  delete, with snapping to real features - rather than as settings in a side form.

Sources: https://help.felt.com/annotations/annotations , https://felt.com/blog/ui-upgrades ,
https://felt.com/blog/felts-new-pricing , https://www.g2.com/products/felt/reviews

### 5.2 Wonderbly - personalization UX

- **What it is:** personalised children's books (and adult gift books).
- **Pricing examples:** My Golden Ticket $39.99 USD [own site]; layflat 11x13 in hardcover $64.99 with
  cover/pages/dedication upgrades adding about $5-10 [3rd party]; Kingdom of You GBP 24.99 on NOTHS.
- **UX patterns worth borrowing:**
  - Minimal input first (child's name, a few avatar choices), then a **full page-by-page preview before
    purchase** ("preview all 38 pages").
  - Avatar options (hair, skin tone) expanded specifically to improve representation and conversion
    (ex-PM case study; metrics withheld).
  - A free-text dedication page as emotional anchor and upsell.
  - Format upsells after the emotional commitment (hardcover, larger size, extra pages).
  - Heavy experimentation culture (Optimizely reports an 18.1% order uplift from one test).
- **For Maploom:** the buyer is a gift-giver, the user is a child. Sell the reveal moment and show the
  entire finished artwork before asking for payment.

Sources: https://wonderbly.com/personalized-products/roald-dahl-my-golden-ticket-book ,
https://medium.com/serenawrites/case-study-transforming-a-customer-personalisation-journey-c82eebb888e0 ,
https://econsultancy.com/how-wonderbly-uses-data-and-personalisation-to-create-a-magical-ecommerce-experience/ ,

## 7. Gaps in the market

1. **Illustrated and automated barely exists.** Automated products are almost all minimalist line
   maps; illustrated products are almost all hand-made, slow and $150-$600. Only Little Roads Co
   (rug only, US) and AI tools like CartoSketch sit in between.
2. **Landmarks are missing from automated maps.** Hand-drawn sellers win on "your house, your school,
   your favourite shops". No automated poster vendor was found offering recognised, named, editable
   points of interest from map data.
3. **One design, one product.** Little Roads sells a rug; poster companies sell wall art. Nobody found
   sells the same personalised neighborhood artwork as rug + poster + canvas + puzzle + digital file.
4. **Kid-oriented map art outside the US.** Little Roads advertises US printing and free US shipping
   only. No EU/UK equivalent was found (absence of evidence only - not an exhaustive search).
5. **Home-address privacy is unaddressed.** No competitor snippet mentioned how entered addresses are
   stored, nor an option to avoid marking the exact home. Public galleries (Maposaic) and Etsy proofs
   are the opposite of private.
6. **Attribution and licensing are handled quietly.** OSM community threads criticise poster sellers
   for hidden credit. A clearly credited, ODbL-compliant product is a small but real trust signal.
7. **Accuracy vs. charm is unresolved.** Little Roads disclaims geographic accuracy; AI tools risk
   invented streets. There is room for "real streets, illustrated charm" with deterministic geometry.
8. **The plain poster is commoditised.** $1 exports and free open-source generators mean a line-map
   poster cannot carry a margin on the artwork alone. Value has to come from illustration, landmarks,
   product range and gifting experience.
9. **Seasonal capacity.** Hand-drawn sellers publish Christmas cutoffs weeks ahead and close
   commissions for months. An automated product with fast fulfilment can take late gift demand.
10. **Schools, nurseries and estate agents** appear only as incidental audiences (Little Roads has a
    classroom-themed page; Etsy sellers mention realtor gifts). No vendor found targets them directly.

## 8. Recommendations for Maploom

1. **Lead with the illustrated, kid-friendly style and do not ship a minimalist line map as the hero.**
   The line-map segment is crowded and now effectively free. Differentiate on warm, toy-like
   illustration with deterministic geometry from OSM so streets stay true.

2. **Build landmark intelligence as the core feature.** Automatically detect and rank nearby OSM POIs
   that matter to a family (school, playground, park, fire station, library, supermarket, church,
   station, water) and render each as an illustrated icon with a label. This is the one thing
   hand-drawn sellers offer that automated vendors do not.

3. **Make landmarks editable like annotations (Felt pattern).** Click to rename ("Grandma's house"),
   hide, move, or add a custom pin. Cap the count for legibility and auto-declutter. OSM coverage is
   uneven, so manual add/rename is necessary, not optional.

4. **Design a multi-product engine from day one.** One scene description, many outputs: rug/play mat,
   poster, canvas, puzzle, digital file. Each needs its own aspect ratio, bleed, minimum line width and
   label size (a rug needs roads wide enough for toy cars; a puzzle needs detail everywhere). Launch
   with one or two products but keep the renderer product-agnostic.

5. **Offer a low-priced digital entry product.** Competitors show a working ladder: $1-$29 digital
   file, about $45-$50 poster, $99+ canvas, $199-$299 rug. A paid high-res download (or a free
   watermarked preview) lets buyers commit before the expensive physical item and serves markets where
   we have no print partner yet.

6. **Show the full artwork before payment and frame it as a gift reveal (Wonderbly pattern).** Address
   in, complete preview out, then personalise (family name, child's name, dedication line, colour
   palette), then choose product. Keep the first screen to a single address field.

7. **Adopt an explicit privacy posture for home addresses.** Geocode without requiring an account,
   do not store the raw address longer than needed for the order, never publish customer maps in a
   gallery by default, and offer "centre on my street, don't mark my house". State this plainly near
   the address field - no competitor was seen doing so.

8. **Render from OSM data directly and credit it visibly.** Avoids Mapbox print-licensing uncertainty
   and export quotas, and matches what Mapiful, Positive Prints and Alvar Carto do. Put
   "(c) OpenStreetMap contributors" in the web UI and discreetly on the product or its packaging/insert.
   Have the ODbL "produced work" position reviewed legally before launch.

9. **Promise "real streets, playful drawing" and handle accuracy honestly.** Keep road geometry real,
   simplify deliberately, and say so. If generative AI is used at all, confine it to texture/decoration
   rather than geometry, so the map never invents roads or buildings. Add a "something wrong? we'll
   fix it" correction loop, since OSM data may be outdated for new-build areas.

10. **Merchandise by occasion and by place.** Occasion templates (new home, new baby, moving away,
    grandparents' house, first school) following the MixPlaces pattern, plus programmatic city/town
    landing pages following the Little Roads pattern, for search traffic. Write all copy and art
    originally.

11. **Target the unserved geography first.** No kid-focused custom-neighborhood product was found for
    Europe/UK. Metric sizes, local print-on-demand partners and non-US address quality (OSM is often
    strongest in Europe) could be a defensible starting market. Validate with a manual check of EU
    competitors, which this research did not cover exhaustively.

12. **Plan for seasonality and post-purchase edits.** Publish clear order-by dates, keep production
    automated enough to accept late orders, and allow small edits after purchase before printing
    (praised in an Atlas.co review). Consider B2B bundles later: nurseries, schools, estate agents.
